# Ownership and Energy Efficiency in Indonesian Manufacturing
**Authors:** Eric D. Ramstetter, Dionisius Narjoko | **Year:** 2014 | **Journal:** Bulletin of Indonesian Economic Studies, 50(2): 255–276 (doi:10.1080/00074918.2014.938407)

## 1. Research Question
Do foreign multinational enterprises (MNEs) and state-owned enterprises (SOEs) use purchased energy more efficiently than local private plants in Indonesian manufacturing? And does the presence of MNEs or SOEs in energy-intensive industries generate energy-efficiency spillovers to local private plants? The motivation is that ownership differentials in energy efficiency — if present — would have policy implications for investment attraction and state enterprise reform.

## 2. Audience
Industrial economics; international business / FDI spillovers; development economics; Indonesian manufacturing studies; energy policy.

## 3. Method / Identification Strategy
Descriptive comparison of energy intensity levels across ownership categories (foreign MNE majority, foreign MNE minority, wholly foreign, SOE, private domestic) in 1996 and 2006. Regression analysis using OLS to estimate energy intensity as a function of plant characteristics including ownership dummies. Spillover analysis: regresses private plant energy intensity on share of output or employment accounted for by MNEs and SOEs within the same industry, controlling for other plant-level variables. Proxy for electricity quantity: since total energy quantity is not measurable, electricity quantity is used as a proxy for total energy (following Eskeland and Harrison 2003). Uses two census years to assess whether patterns are stable or changed over the decade.

## 4. Data
- **Source:** Census of Indonesian medium-large manufacturing plants (BPS Indonesia), 1996 and 2006
- **Unit of observation:** Plant (medium and large manufacturing establishments)
- **Sample size / N:** Not specified explicitly in the extracted text; described as "census data" covering all medium-large plants across Indonesian manufacturing
- **Time period:** Two cross-sections: 1996 and 2006
- **Coverage:** Indonesian manufacturing sector; multiple industries disaggregated by ISIC; three energy types analysed — total energy (proxied by electricity quantity), gas intensity, and coal intensity

## 5. Statistical Methods
- OLS regressions of ln(energy intensity) on ownership dummies and plant controls (labour, intermediate inputs excluding energy, energy quantity)
- R² varies substantially: 0.59 or higher for total energy and coal in 1996; as low as 0.18 (total energy in other transportation machinery, 2006) and 0.36 or lower for gas intensity in both years
- Spillover regressions regress private plant energy intensity on within-industry MNE and SOE shares
- Coefficients on labour and energy quantity are generally positive and significant; coefficients on intermediate consumption (excluding own energy) are negative and significant
- Specifications follow Ramstetter and Narjoko (2012) appendix tables 3, 6, 7 for detailed results

## 6. Key Findings
- The correlation between plant ownership (MNE, SOE, local private) and total energy intensity, gas intensity, and coal intensity was **generally weak** in both 1996 and 2006 — there is no consistent pattern of MNEs or SOEs being more energy-efficient than local private plants
- In 1996, private energy intensities were often positively correlated with SOE presence and majority-foreign MNE presence, but negatively correlated with wholly-foreign and minority-foreign MNE presence
- In 2006, these patterns differed substantially from 1996 — suggesting no stable spillover relationship
- Ownership-related differentials in energy intensity and intra-industry energy intensity spillovers are **not pronounced**
- Workers with secondary education were positively and significantly correlated with total energy intensities in slightly under half of estimates; other technological sophistication indicators were generally not significant
- Policy implication: plant ownership should not be a major consideration in energy efficiency policy for Indonesian manufacturing

## 7. Contributions
- Provides a systematic ownership-disaggregated analysis of energy efficiency in Indonesian manufacturing across two decades
- Tests the FDI energy-efficiency spillover hypothesis directly using plant-level census data for Indonesia
- One of few papers to examine energy intensity differentials by state vs. private vs. foreign ownership simultaneously at plant level in a developing-country context
- Covers 1996 (pre-Asian financial crisis) and 2006 (post-crisis recovery), enabling comparison across a major structural break

## 8. Replication Feasibility
- **Data access:** BPS Indonesia manufacturing census (Sensus Ekonomi / Survei Tahunan Industri Pengolahan) — census data not publicly accessible; requires BPS agreement or institutional collaboration
- **Code availability:** No replication archive cited; detailed appendix tables referenced as Ramstetter and Narjoko (2012) working paper
- **Barriers:** Census-level manufacturing data has restricted access; electricity quantity proxy assumption (following Eskeland and Harrison 2003) introduces measurement uncertainty; results from two-census cross-section limit causal inference
