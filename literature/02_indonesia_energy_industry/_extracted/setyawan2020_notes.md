# Energy Efficiency in Indonesia's Manufacturing Industry: A Perspective from Log Mean Divisia Index Decomposition Analysis
**Authors:** Dhani Setyawan | **Year:** 2020 | **Journal:** Global Journal of Environmental Science and Management, 6(3): 385–402 (Open Access)

## 1. Research Question
What are the trends and determinants of energy intensity change in Indonesia's manufacturing sector from 1980 to 2015, and to what extent are changes driven by within-industry efficiency improvements versus structural shifts toward less energy-intensive industries? The paper focuses on a 35-year period spanning rapid industrialisation, the 1997–98 Asian financial crisis, and the post-crisis recovery, providing historical context lacking in prior Indonesian studies.

## 2. Audience
Energy economics; industrial policy; environmental policy; researchers studying energy efficiency decomposition in developing-country manufacturing; Indonesia specialists.

## 3. Method / Identification Strategy
Index Decomposition Analysis (IDA) using the Log Mean Divisia Index II (LMDI II) method. Decomposes aggregate manufacturing energy intensity into: (1) an intensity effect (within-industry energy efficiency changes weighted by sectoral output shares) and (2) a structural effect (shifts in industry composition toward more or less energy-intensive sub-sectors). No causal identification — purely an accounting/decomposition exercise. LMDI II is preferred over Divisia I and other decomposition methods for its completeness (no residual) and consistency in aggregation (Ang 2004, 2005). Analysis covers 9 manufacturing sub-sectors based on ISIC classification.

## 4. Data
- **Source:** Indonesian Statistical Bureau (BPS) — Series of Indices of Manufacturing Industry; supplemented with energy balance data; details on specific BPS publication years cited in references
- **Unit of observation:** Manufacturing sub-sector (9 ISIC sub-sectors aggregated)
- **Sample size / N:** Sector-level time series (not firm-level); 9 sub-sectors over 36 annual observations
- **Time period:** 1980–2015 (35 years)
- **Coverage:** Indonesia's manufacturing sector; 9 sub-sectors: food (31), textile (32), wood (33), paper (34), chemicals (35), non-metallic minerals (36), basic metals (37), fabricated metals (38), and other

## 5. Statistical Methods
- LMDI II decomposition of aggregate energy intensity index (E_t/E_0) into intensity effect index and structural effect index
- No regression analysis — purely decomposition accounting
- Period analysis divided into three sub-periods: pre-crisis (1980–1997), crisis (1997–2000), post-crisis (2000–2015); and the full period (1980–2015)
- Aggregate energy intensity expressed as ratio relative to base year

## 6. Key Findings
- **Overall (1980–2015):** Aggregate manufacturing energy intensity declined 65% over 35 years
  - Within-industry intensity effect: −62% contribution (dominant driver)
  - Structural shift effect: −9% contribution (minor driver)
- **Pre-crisis period (1980–1997):** Both intensity and structural effects declined significantly; efficiency improved especially in textile (32), non-metallic minerals (36), and chemicals (35)
- **Crisis period (1997–2000):** Energy intensity increased. Within-industry intensity effect increased (worsened) as output fell faster than energy consumption. Basic metals sector showed the greatest increase in intensity effect. Structural effect contribution became smaller relative to within-industry changes.
- **Post-crisis period (2000–2015):** Energy intensity resumed its decline. Intensity effects reduced aggregate energy intensity by ~12% in both 2000–2010 and 2010–2015 sub-periods. Food, non-metallic minerals, and basic metals experienced substantial decreases in intensity effect post-2000. Structural effect also decreased (positive direction) in most sectors.
- **Structural shifts:** Food (31) and fabricated metals (38) are the two largest value-added contributors and also have the smallest energy intensities. Basic metals (37) showed the greatest intensity effect increase during the crisis.
- **Non-metallic minerals (36)** (cement/lime) consistently the most energy-intensive sector.
- Policy note: Government Regulation No. 70/2009 on energy conservation and energy subsidy reform are credited with post-2009 efficiency improvements.

## 7. Contributions
- First Indonesian manufacturing energy intensity study using LMDI II decomposition specifically; prior work used simpler decomposition methods
- Provides a comprehensive 35-year historical analysis covering multiple economic phases (boom, crisis, recovery)
- Demonstrates that within-industry efficiency improvements dominate structural shifts in explaining Indonesia's energy intensity trends — structural policy alone is insufficient
- Offers sub-sector granularity useful for targeting energy efficiency interventions (cement/lime as priority)

## 8. Replication Feasibility
- **Data access:** BPS Series of Indices of Manufacturing Industry — publicly available from BPS Indonesia website; energy balance data from Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM)
- **Code availability:** Author states data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request; no formal replication archive
- **Barriers:** Constructing consistent 35-year sub-sector energy and output series requires reconciling multiple BPS publication vintages and sector classification changes; LMDI II computation is straightforward with standard spreadsheet tools
