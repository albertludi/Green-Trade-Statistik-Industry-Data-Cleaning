# Energy and Emission Changes in Industrial Sectors: Evidence from Indonesia
**Authors:** Rezky K. Zain, Arianto A. Patunru, Djoni Hartono | **Year:** 2025 | **Journal:** Energy Sources, Part B: Economics, Planning, and Policy, 20(1): 2532161 (doi:10.1080/15567249.2025.2532161)

## 1. Research Question
Which Indonesian industrial sectors have the highest lifecycle energy and emission intensity (from a final-demand perspective), and what are the main drivers of energy and emission changes between 2010 and 2016? The paper uses a lifecycle/embodied-energy lens to identify "hidden" energy-intensive sectors that production-based accounting would miss, and to attribute emission changes to economic growth, structural change, and technology effects.

## 2. Audience
Energy economics; environmental input-output analysis; climate policy; Indonesian sectoral energy/emissions analysis; researchers using hybrid energy IO and structural decomposition.

## 3. Method / Identification Strategy
Two-part analytical framework. First, constructs a Hybrid Energy Input-Output (HEIO) table for Indonesia (2010 and 2016) by replacing monetary energy transactions in the IO table with physical units (TJ), preserving energy conservation principles and avoiding double-counting of emissions. This enables tracing both direct and indirect (embodied) energy and emissions through value chains to final demand. Second, applies Structural Decomposition Analysis (SDA) using two-polar decomposition to decompose energy and emission changes into: (i) direct energy intensity, (ii) technology structure (embodied energy), (iii) net export final demand, and (iv) domestic final demand effects. No causal identification strategy — this is an accounting/decomposition exercise.

## 4. Data
- **Source:** IO Tables of Indonesia 2010 and 2016 (BPS); energy consumption data by sector (BPS 2015, 2021a — 64 non-energy sectors × 19 energy products); cross-checked against ESDM Handbook of Energy and Economic Statistics (HEES) and APEC EGEDA Indonesia Energy Balance; emission factors from IPCC (2006) Guidelines
- **Unit of observation:** Industrial sector / cluster (83-sector IO, aggregated to 14 non-energy clusters for presentation)
- **Sample size / N:** 83-sector HEIO table (7 primary energy + 12 secondary energy + 64 non-energy sectors); two IO table years
- **Time period:** 2010 and 2016
- **Coverage:** Full Indonesian economy; 83 sectors; three GHG gases (CO2, CH4, N2O converted to CO2e); energy content and emission factors from published sources (Tables 1 and 2 in paper)

## 5. Statistical Methods
- HEIO construction: replaces monetary energy flows with physical TJ; Leontief inverse applied to derive total energy requirement matrix (direct + indirect)
- Direct energy intensity (αe) and indirect/embodied energy intensity (αne) separated following Kagawa and Inamura (2001) / Ueda (2022) methodology
- Two-polar SDA decomposition (approximation of full polar decomposition); four drivers: energy/carbon intensity, structural (technology) change, net export effect, domestic demand effect
- Emission SDA avoids double-counting by omitting primary-to-secondary energy flows in the modified direct requirement matrix A*
- Cluster weighted averages used for presentation (output-weighted)
- No econometric estimation — pure IO accounting

## 6. Key Findings
- **Energy intensity by cluster (2016, GJ/million constant 2015 IDR final demand):**
  - Non-metallic minerals: 3.84 (highest)
  - Metals and metal products: 1.84
  - Pulp, paper and wood products: 1.05
  - Construction: 1.14
  - Transportation: 0.65
  - Services: 0.27
- **Indirect (embodied) energy share:** Extractive clusters (non-energy mining) have 90% indirect energy in 2016; transport equipment has 79% indirect (0.52 GJ indirect vs. 0.14 GJ direct per million IDR); construction has 65% indirect
- **Non-metallic minerals and metals** have 70–90% primary energy use (requiring direct combustion for high-temperature processes)
- **SDA energy changes (2010–2016):** Economic development (final demand) accounts for 71% of total energy increase; energy mix shift (to coal and petroleum) accounts for 27%; structural change only 2%
- **SDA emission changes (2010–2016):** Total additional emissions = 111.9 million tCO2e. Domestic demand drove +114.5 million tCO2e (102%); structural change +24.5 million; carbon intensity improvement and net export reduction together offset −27.5 million
- **Electricity sector** contributed 35% of total emission increase (111.9 million tCO2e) due to 80 TWh consumption growth met 86% by coal power plants
- **Coal emissions:** domestic demand alone caused +52.8 million tCO2e; combined with technology deterioration adds +52.5 million tCO2e more; offset by −8.8 million from net export reduction

## 7. Contributions
- First study to construct and use HEIO for Indonesia — avoids EEIO proportionality assumption and maintains energy conservation principles
- Uses Indonesia's most disaggregated IO tables (83 sectors) for energy-emission analysis, providing greater sector detail than prior IO studies
- Uncovers "hidden" energy- and emission-intensive sectors (transport equipment, construction) that are missed by production-based accounting
- Provides emission factor tables (IPCC-based, Table 2) and energy content factors (Table 1) useful for other researchers working with Indonesian energy data
- Offers policy-differentiated decarbonisation pathways: electrification for downstream/secondary-energy sectors vs. hydrogen/carbon pricing for high-temperature primary-energy sectors

## 8. Replication Feasibility
- **Data access:** IO Tables 2010 and 2016 publicly available from BPS Indonesia; energy data from BPS and ESDM HEES (publicly available); IPCC emission factors are standard
- **Code availability:** Concordance table available upon request from authors; detailed sectoral results available upon request
- **Barriers:** Harmonising IO sector classifications across two IO table releases requires a concordance table (available from authors); constructing HEIO from monetary IO and physical energy data is methodologically non-trivial; 2020 IO Table released January 2025 but without energy data as of writing
