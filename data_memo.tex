\section{A.\;Dataset Overview}

The Statistik Industri (SI) — also known as the Industri Besar dan Sedang (IBS) survey — is
Indonesia's annual census of medium and large manufacturing establishments. It is conducted by
BPS (Badan Pusat Statistik) and covers all establishments with 20 or more workers.

\textbf{Unit of observation:} one row = one establishment in one year.

\textbf{Key identifiers:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \texttt{psid} — Plant/establishment ID (panel identifier)
  \item \texttt{year} — Survey year
  \item \texttt{disic5} / \texttt{disic4} — 5-digit and 4-digit industry code (ISIC)
  \item \texttt{dprovi} — Province code
  \item \texttt{ekspor} — Export status (1 = exporter, 2 = non-exporter; non-missing obs count to be confirmed$^*$)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Variable List and Naming Convention}

\texttt{ef} marks the electricity variable; \texttt{z} marks fuel variables;

quantities. Variable labels are \textit{all missing} in the raw \texttt{.dta} file;
fuel-type mappings below are inferred from IBS conventions and must be verified against the

\noindent\textbf{Note on electricity variables:} \texttt{efuvcu} is \textit{not} the sum of the fuel
variables; it is the \textbf{total purchased electricity expenditure} =
\texttt{eplvcu} (PLN) + \texttt{enpvcu} (non-PLN). This is confirmed by a median ratio of
\texttt{efuvcu}/(\texttt{eplvcu}+\texttt{enpvcu}) $= 0.997 \approx 1.0$ across observations
where both components are positive. The dataset therefore replicates the PLN/non-PLN
breakdown from the questionnaire. Non-PLN electricity is sparse (3.8\% of obs),
consistent with most Indonesian manufacturers purchasing exclusively from the state grid.
No corresponding variable was found in the dataset for self-generated electricity
(questionnaire code \texttt{ESGKHU}) or electricity sold to others (\texttt{YELVCU}/\texttt{OELKHU}).

Table~\ref{tab:fuelmaster} tracks all fuel items across every survey period from 2000 to 2014.
The column headers correspond to distinct questionnaire forms; the key changes across periods are
as follows.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{2000}: Full set of liquid and solid fuel items collected individually.
        Coal and gas/LPG each have their own rows. No production/non-production split.

  \item \textbf{01--02}: Major simplification --- kerosene, coal, coke, gas/LPG, and fuel oil
        were collapsed into a single ``bahan bakar lainnya'' (other fuels) row.
        Only diesel, gasoline, other fuels, and lubricants were collected as separate items.
        This explains lower physical-quantity coverage for kerosene and coal in those years.

  \item \textbf{2003}: Individual items restored. Kerosene, coal, gas~PGN, and LPG reappear
        as separate rows.

  \item \textbf{2004--2005}: Structure similar to 2003, with one important exception:
        coal is recorded in \textbf{Ton} rather than Kg. Gas~PGN and LPG were
        subsumed into ``other fuels'' in 2004 and reappear separately in 2005.

  \item \textbf{2006}: The 2006 Economic Census form (\textit{SE06-UMB-D}) covers more items
        than the regular annual IBS form --- IDO, Bunker~C, Coke, Charcoal, and Firewood appear
        as explicit rows. Coal unit returns to Kg.

  \item \textbf{07--11}: Stable-core form. Eight fuel items collected with both physical
        quantity and monetary value columns, plus a generator-only sub-column for each.
        Production/non-production split introduced for the first time (\texttt{zp*}/\texttt{zn*}).
        Coal unit is Kg throughout.

  \item \textbf{2010}: Structural exception. Monetary values only (no physical quantities),
        no production/non-production split, and a different \texttt{IF*} code convention.
        Several items are merged: all solid fuels into one row, gas~PGN and LPG combined.

  \item \textbf{12--14}: Two new items added --- Briket Batubara and Gas non-PGN.
        Otherwise consistent with the stable-core structure.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Analytical notes}
\label{sec:analyticalnotes}

\noindent\textbf{Question 1: Do coverage gaps reflect items the questionnaire stopped collecting?}

\noindent\textbf{Confirmation:} Yes, partially.
In 2001 and 2002 the questionnaire was simplified from ten distinct fuel items to six by
\textit{collapsing} kerosene, coal, coke, gas/LPG, and fuel oil into a single
``bahan bakar lainnya (other fuels)'' line. As a result, neither the physical quantity
nor the monetary value of these fuels was collected separately in those two years ---
both dimensions were subsumed into the combined ``other fuels'' row.
This is consistent with lower \texttt{kcu} coverage for kerosene (\texttt{zpskcu}: 5.9\%) and
coal (\texttt{zpikcu}: 5.8\%) relative to diesel (\texttt{zpdkcu}: 11.5\%).
One complication: despite the 2001--02 collapse (which suppressed both quantity and
value for kerosene on the questionnaire), the monetary value series \texttt{zpsvcu} runs
continuously 1990--2014 in the compiled dataset, while the ``other fuel'' value variable
(\texttt{zpxvcu}) only begins in 2005. Because the questionnaire did not collect kerosene
value separately in 2001--02, the uninterrupted \texttt{zpsvcu} series implies that the
dataset compiler either imputed these values or used a supplementary source not captured
in the annual form. This is an open question that should be investigated before using
pre-2003 kerosene values.

\noindent\textbf{Question 2: Did physical quantity units change across survey waves?}

\noindent\textbf{Confirmation:} Yes, for coal only.
Coal is the only fuel with a documented unit change: questionnaires for 2000, 2003, and 2006 (SE06)
record coal in Kg, while the 2004 and 2005 questionnaires both use Ton.
The 2007-onward stable form also uses Kg.
The coal unit sequence is therefore: Kg (pre-2004) $\to$ Ton (2004--2005) $\to$ Kg (2006+).
The \texttt{zpikcu}/\texttt{znikcu} series contains an implicit scale break covering the
2004 and 2005 waves. Multiply 2004--2005 observations by 1{,}000 to convert Ton $\to$ Kg
before use.
All other fuel units (diesel, kerosene, gasoline in litres) appear stable across waves.

\noindent\textbf{Question 3: Is the production/non-production split identifiable before 2007?}

\noindent\textbf{Confirmation:} The split is absent before 2007.
None of the questionnaires from 2000 through 2006 contain a production-use versus
non-production-use breakdown for fuels. Both the 2004 annual form and the SE06 census form
record a single ``Seluruhnya'' (total) column per fuel ---
but no \texttt{zp}/\texttt{zn} distinction.
The dataset's \texttt{zp*}/\texttt{zn*} variable pairs therefore have no questionnaire-level
counterpart before 2007; it is unclear whether the pre-2007 \texttt{zp*} values were
imputed, copied from the total, or left zero in the compiled panel.

\noindent\textbf{2. The dataset follows a systematic internal naming convention,
independent of BPS codes.}

Dataset fuel variables are named: \texttt{Z} + \{\texttt{P} = production $\mid$
\texttt{N} = non-production\} + \textit{fuel letter} + \{\texttt{VCU} = value (Rp~000)
$\mid$ \texttt{KCU} = physical quantity\}, where \texttt{d} = diesel, \texttt{s} =
kerosene, \texttt{o} = gasoline, \texttt{i} = coal, \texttt{z} = gas/LPG, \texttt{x} =
other. BPS independently reused similar letter sequences for unrelated purposes ---
for instance, \texttt{ZPSVCU}/\texttt{ZNSVCU} for wages in 2005 and 2009--2011 ---
creating apparent collisions that are purely coincidental.

\textit{Concrete illustration:} In the 2005, 2009, and 2011 questionnaires,
\texttt{ZPSVCU} is the code for \textit{base wage of production workers}, yet the
dataset variable \texttt{zpsvcu} records kerosene (production use).
The two naming systems are entirely independent; resolving the full mapping requires
the BPS codebook for the compiled SI panel.

\noindent\textbf{3. Year-by-year variable availability in the dataset (2000--2014).}

Table~\ref{tab:availability} checks whether each dataset variable has at least one

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Gasoline and coal gaps (2001--05, 2007--09)}: \texttt{zpovcu} and
        \texttt{zpivcu} are fully absent in eight years (2001--05 and 2007--09) despite the questionnaire
        collecting these items. This is unexpected and requires investigation ---
        the compiled dataset may store these values under a different variable,
        or they may have been zeroed during compilation.
  \item \textbf{Production/non-production split}: \texttt{zn*vcu} variables are
        available in exactly the same years as \texttt{zp*vcu} --- including years
        before 2007 when the questionnaire had no \texttt{zp/zn} distinction.
        Pre-2007 values were likely imputed or copied from the total.
  \item \textbf{Gas/LPG (\texttt{zpzvcu})}: available 2001--2009 (skipping 2006),
        consistent with Table~\ref{tab:fuelmaster}.
  \item \textbf{Other fuels (\texttt{zpxvcu})}: present only in 2005, 2008--09;
        effectively unusable as a panel series.
\end{itemize}

  title={\color{warnred}\textbf{Need to Decide}},
  fonttitle=\bfseries, colbacktitle=red!10!white, coltitle=warnred,
  titlerule=0.5pt]
How to treat \texttt{zpxvcu}/\texttt{znxvcu} (``other fuels'') in the estimation.
Three options:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Exclude entirely.} \texttt{zpxvcu} is non-zero in only three years
        (2005, 2008--09); its average share of total fuel expenditure in those years
        is $\approx$2--3\% (mean) / $\approx$1\% (median). Exclusion introduces
        minimal bias for most firm-years.
  \item \textbf{Include with a caveat.} Retain \texttt{zpxvcu} where non-zero and
        note that it represents a small residual. Suitable if completeness matters
        more than clean identification.
  \item \textbf{Attempt to estimate carbon content.} Requires an assumed emission
        factor for the ``other'' category, but the variable is a \textit{mixture}
        of IDO, Bunker~C, Coke, Charcoal, Firewood, Gas non-PGN, and potentially
        Lubricants --- each with very different carbon intensities.
        Any single emission factor would be highly uncertain.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{1em}

\begin{tcolorbox}[
  colframe=warnamber, colback=yellow!12!white,
  boxrule=1.2pt, arc=3pt,
  left=8pt, right=8pt, top=6pt, bottom=6pt,
  title={\color{warnamber}\textbf{Need to Confirm}},
  fonttitle=\bfseries, colbacktitle=yellow!20!white, coltitle=warnamber,
  titlerule=0.5pt]
Physical quantity unit consistency across survey years for the 5 usable fuel variables
(relevant when converting \texttt{kcu} to carbon):
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Coal (\texttt{zpikcu}):} Unit changes Kg $\to$ \textbf{Ton} (2004--05)
        $\to$ Kg (2006+). Confirmed from questionnaires. Action: multiply 2004--05
        observations by 1{,}000 before use.
  \item \textbf{Gas + LPG (\texttt{zpzkcu}):} Questionnaire collects Gas~PGN in M$^3$
        and LPG in Kg separately, but the dataset aggregates both into one variable.
        Unit is ambiguous --- we cannot determine the gas/LPG split without additional
        data, making carbon conversion unreliable for this variable.
  \item \textbf{Diesel / Kerosene / Gasoline:} Confirmed in Liters across all
        questionnaires checked (2000--05, 2006 SE06, 2007--11, 2012--14).
        Unit is stable throughout the full panel.
  \item \textbf{Electricity (\texttt{efuvcu}):} Present in the dataset but has no
        variable label (all labels are blank in \texttt{data8514\_SI.dta}).
        Description confirmed through our questionnaire analysis:
        \texttt{efuvcu} = \texttt{eplvcu} (PLN) + \texttt{enpvcu} (non-PLN) =
        total purchased electricity expenditure (Rp~000), verified empirically
        (median ratio $\approx 1.0$).
        Physical quantity in KWh is not recorded in the dataset.
        To convert to carbon emissions, KWh must first be imputed by dividing
        \texttt{efuvcu} by the annual PLN electricity tariff
        (Rp per KWh, from BPS/PLN price series):
        $\text{KWh} = \texttt{efuvcu} \div \text{tariff}$.
        The resulting KWh can then be multiplied by the PLN grid emission factor
        (kg CO$_2$/KWh) to obtain carbon-equivalent emissions.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{E.\;Physical Quantity Variables and the Sparsity Problem}
\label{sec:sparse}

Each fuel variable has a physical quantity counterpart (\texttt{kcu} suffix) alongside the
monetary value (Panel~C of Table~\ref{tab:coverage}). These are the variables needed to
compute carbon emissions directly. However, they are very sparse (1--12\% non-zero)
and are only available for 1985--2006; there are no \texttt{kcu} observations from
2007 onward.

\noindent\textbf{Implication for carbon conversion:} Because \texttt{kcu} variables are sparse,
the full-panel carbon emission measure will need to rely on the monetary \texttt{vcu} values
combined with \textbf{fuel price deflators} (BPS/Pertamina/PLN annual price series by fuel
type) to back out physical quantities. This adds a step to the pipeline.


% ============================================================
\section{F.\;Planned Cleaning Pipeline}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Verify variable definitions} against questionnaire PDFs in the data folder
        (years 2000–2014 are available). \textit{Cross-check during meeting.}
  \item \textbf{Check cross-year consistency}: the SI questionnaire form changed over time;
        some fuel categories may be merged or split across survey waves.
        \textit{Cross-check using a line chart of average value per variable by year ---
        a structural break or implausible jump signals a form change or unit shift.}
  \item \textbf{Harmonise monetary values}: confirm units are Rp000; deflate to a base year
        using CPI (variable \texttt{cpi} is present in the dataset).
        \textit{Cross-check using a line chart of mean deflated value by year to confirm
        no residual discontinuities after deflation.}
        One additional diagnostic of interest: plot the ratio
        \texttt{vtlvcu} (value added) / \texttt{yprvcu} (gross output) over time ---
        an implausibly flat or erratic series would signal measurement issues in either
        the energy or output variables.
        \textit{Outlier/anomaly correction to be handled using machine learning
        (approach to be confirmed further).}
  \item \textbf{Construct physical quantity series}: use fuel price deflators to convert
        \texttt{vcu} → physical units where \texttt{kcu} is missing.
        \textit{Price deflators to be sourced from literature review and/or official
        government documents (BPS/Pertamina/PLN price series).}
  \item \textbf{Apply emission conversion factors}: multiply physical quantities by
        fuel-specific $\text{CO}_2$ equivalent factors.
        \textit{Emission factors to be determined from literature review.}
  \item \textbf{Compute carbon intensity}: total $\text{CO}_2$-equivalent / output value.
  \item \textbf{Save clean dataset}: \texttt{output/energy\_clean\_0014.dta}.
\end{enumerate}
